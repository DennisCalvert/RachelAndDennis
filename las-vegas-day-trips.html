Beyond Las Vegas

Ghost towns, abandoned mining history, desert sculpture, ancient rock art, and cinematic landscapes — mostly within a two-hour drive of the city.

For photographers, filmmakers, history lovers, and anyone who wants something beyond the Strip. Driving times are approximate from central Las Vegas, one way, and depend on traffic and conditions.

Destinations worth the drive

45–60 minutes

1. Nelson Ghost Town & Eldorado Canyon

Rusted vehicles, old wooden buildings, mining equipment, and dramatic desert Americana. A particularly strong choice for staged-looking photographs and weathered textures.

Photography · Mining history · Film-set atmosphere

Much of the photogenic property is privately managed. Arrange photography or mine-tour access in advance; don't enter structures or mines without permission.

1 hr 50 min–2 hr 15 min

2. Rhyolite Ghost Town

A former mining boomtown near Beatty with stone ruins, the remains of a bank, and a historic railroad depot. More authentic abandoned-settlement atmosphere than a curated attraction.

Ruins · History · Architectural photography

Explore from permitted areas and stay out of unstable buildings and mine workings.

Open map
About 2 hours

3. Goldwell Open Air Museum

Surreal outdoor sculpture beside Rhyolite, including ghostlike figures set against an expansive desert backdrop. Easy to pair with a visit to the ghost town.

Outdoor art · Surreal photography · Desert

Open map
55–75 minutes

4. Valley of Fire State Park

Red sandstone formations, layered rock, dramatic desert scenery, and ancient petroglyphs. A standout for wide-angle compositions and late-afternoon light.

Landscape photography · Geology · Petroglyphs

Check current park access and trail closures before departure; heat and seasonal restrictions can be significant.

Open map
40–50 minutes

5. Goodsprings & Pioneer Saloon

A small historic mining town anchored by a saloon dating to 1913. Old West character, a relaxed lunch stop, and a fun reference point for fans of Fallout: New Vegas.

Historic town · Food · Vintage Americana

Open map
About 1 hr 30 min

6. China Ranch Date Farm

An unexpected palm-filled oasis near Tecopa, California, set among dry desert hills and canyons. Walk the trails and stop for a date shake or bakery treat.

Oasis · Hiking · Unusual landscapes

Open map
About 1 hr 15 min

7. St. Thomas Ghost Town

Remnants of a settlement that spent decades underwater after Lake Mead filled. Exposed foundations and the story of the lost town make this a compelling historical walk.

Lost history · Desert hiking · Archaeological remains

Visibility and trail access can change with lake levels and conditions. Bring water and avoid extreme heat.

These are planning estimates, not live navigation or guaranteed visit durations. The Rhyolite–Goldwell combination is the strongest full-day choice for ruins and art; Nelson works well as a half-day trip.

Before you go

Desert travel essentials: Carry extra water, fuel up before remote stretches, download offline maps, and check road conditions. Stay out of abandoned mine shafts and unstable structures. Respect private property, protected artifacts, and petroglyphs. Confirm operating hours, tour reservations, fees, and seasonal park closures directly before traveling.

Useful official resources: Nevada State Parks — Valley of Fire · National Park Service — Lake Mead · Bureau of Land Management.
